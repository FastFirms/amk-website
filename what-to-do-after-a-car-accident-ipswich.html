The Ipswich Motorway, the Warrego Highway, the Cunningham Highway — and every local street in between. If you've been injured in a car accident in or around Ipswich, here's exactly what to do, in order.

This guide is general information, not legal advice for your specific situation. If you've been injured, the safest first step is a free claim check or a quick call. But if you're reading this from the roadside or the hospital, start here.

1. Get to safety and call 000 if needed

If it's safe to do so, move out of traffic. Switch on your hazard lights and call 000 immediately if anyone is injured or trapped. On a motorway like the M7 through Wacol and Redbank, or on the Cunningham Highway through Booval, getting clear of moving traffic is the priority before anything else.

Don't move an injured person unless they are in immediate danger from fire, traffic or a similar hazard.

2. See a doctor — even if you feel okay

Go to Ipswich Hospital's emergency department on Chelmsford Avenue, North Ipswich, or see your GP within 24 to 48 hours. Adrenaline masks injuries. Whiplash, soft-tissue damage and concussion often don't show until the next day. A medical record created close to the accident date is one of the most important pieces of evidence in any CTP claim — a gap between the accident and your first appointment is one of the first things a CTP insurer will point to when disputing your claim.

When you see the doctor, tell them clearly that your symptoms relate to a motor vehicle accident in Ipswich, and make sure that's documented in your medical record.

3. Exchange details and gather evidence

Collect the other driver's name, licence number, registration, and contact details. Do the same for any witnesses. Then document the scene before anything is moved:

  • The position of all vehicles
  • Damage to every car involved — close-ups and wide shots
  • Skid marks, debris, road surface, and any traffic signs or signals
  • Your visible injuries
  • Any nearby CCTV cameras — businesses on Brisbane Road, service stations on the Warrego Highway, and council cameras at major intersections

Dashcam footage from other drivers is worth asking about on the day. This evidence is often overwritten within 24 to 72 hours.

4. Report to Queensland Police

You must report the accident to police if someone was injured, a vehicle has to be towed, a driver failed to stop or exchange details, or you believe a driver was affected by drugs or alcohol. You can report to Ipswich Police Station on Brisbane Street, or use the online reporting form for minor incidents where nobody was hurt.

For accidents on the Ipswich Motorway or state highways, Queensland Police or Queensland Traffic may already be attending — get the attending officer's details and ask for the report number. That number supports your CTP claim and, for unidentified driver situations, is effectively mandatory for a successful claim against the Nominal Defendant.

5. Know the CTP deadlines

Queensland's Compulsory Third Party scheme is the primary route for compensation after a motor vehicle accident. The deadlines are strict:

Three Deadlines That Matter
  • 28 days — lodge an Accident Notification Form to access early treatment and rehabilitation funding
  • 9 months — file a Notice of Accident Claim Form (or within 1 month of first consulting a lawyer)
  • 3 years — the absolute limit to commence court proceedings

If the other driver cannot be identified — which can happen on stretches of the Warrego Highway, the Cunningham Highway, or quieter Ipswich residential streets — the deadline to notify the Nominal Defendant is just 3 months from the accident. This is much shorter than most people realise. Get legal advice early.

6. Be careful with the insurer

The at-fault driver's CTP insurer may contact you quickly — sometimes within days — to take a recorded statement or offer early settlement. You are not obliged to give a recorded statement without legal advice, and early offers are almost always made before your full injury picture is clear. Once you sign a settlement deed you cannot reopen the claim, even if your condition significantly worsens.

What you can claim after a car accident in Ipswich

A successful CTP claim can cover:

  • Medical expenses — past and future, including specialist treatment, surgery, physiotherapy and rehabilitation
  • Loss of income and superannuation — both wages already lost and projected future earnings if your work capacity is reduced
  • Care and assistance — paid professional care, and the value of unpaid care provided by family members
  • General damages — compensation for pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement

For serious injuries, future economic loss is typically the largest head of damage — and the one CTP insurers contest most aggressively. Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do immediately after a car accident in Ipswich?

Call 000 if anyone is injured, move safely out of traffic, and switch on your hazard lights. Exchange name, licence, registration and contact details with the other driver and any witnesses. Photograph vehicle positions, all damage, road conditions and your visible injuries before anything is moved. If anyone was injured, a vehicle needs towing, or a driver failed to stop, report to Queensland Police. See a doctor within 24 to 48 hours — adrenaline masks many injuries and a gap in your early medical record will be used against you by the CTP insurer.

Which hospital should I go to after a car accident in Ipswich?

Ipswich Hospital on Chelmsford Avenue, North Ipswich has a 24-hour emergency department and is the main public hospital for the region. For less serious injuries you may attend your own GP — but make sure you attend promptly and clearly document that your symptoms relate to a motor vehicle accident. Delaying medical attention weakens your CTP claim.

How long do I have to make a claim after a car accident in Ipswich?

Under Queensland's CTP scheme: lodge an Accident Notification Form within 28 days; file a Notice of Accident Claim Form within 9 months of the accident (or within 1 month of first seeing a lawyer); and commence court proceedings within 3 years. If the other driver cannot be identified — which can occur on the Warrego Highway, Cunningham Highway or quieter Ipswich roads — you must notify the Nominal Defendant within just 3 months. These are strict cut-offs. Missing them can end your right to claim entirely.

What roads in Ipswich have the most accidents?

The Ipswich Motorway (M7) through Wacol, Redbank and Bundamba is one of Queensland's busiest freight corridors and sees frequent crashes. The Warrego Highway through Yamanto and Leichhardt and the Cunningham Highway through Booval are also common accident sites. Local Ipswich intersections — particularly around Brisbane Road, Limestone Street and the CBD — are frequent locations for lower-speed collisions. The same CTP rules apply regardless of where in Ipswich your accident occurred.

Do I have to accept the CTP insurer's first offer?

No. Early settlement offers are made before your full injury picture is known and are almost always below what a fully settled claim is worth. Once you sign a settlement deed, you generally cannot reopen the claim even if your condition worsens. Get independent legal advice first.
