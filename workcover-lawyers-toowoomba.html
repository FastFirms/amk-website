01

How Do Toowoomba Workers Lodge A WorkCover Claim?

Report the injury to your employer, get a work capacity certificate from your doctor, then lodge with WorkCover Queensland within 6 months of the injury or diagnosis.

Lodging promptly matters even when an injury seems minor at first. Back strains from lifting grain bags or livestock, shoulder injuries from repetitive processing work and knee injuries on building sites often worsen over weeks, and an early claim keeps your options open.

AMK can check your claim form, make sure the injury is described accurately and confirm your employer has been notified. See our WorkCover claims page for how the Queensland scheme works across the state.

02

What Weekly Payments Can A Toowoomba Worker Receive?

Once a claim is accepted, WorkCover Queensland pays weekly compensation while you cannot work, plus reasonable medical, rehabilitation and travel costs.

Seasonal and shift-based work is common across the Darling Downs, so the way your normal weekly earnings are calculated can make a real difference to what you receive. Check the figure carefully when your first payment arrives.

If your payments are reduced or stopped because WorkCover forms the view that you can return to work, that decision is reviewable. Strong, up-to-date medical evidence is usually what turns it around.

03

What If WorkCover Rejects Your Toowoomba Claim?

You can ask for a review of the decision, generally within 3 months. Many rejections rest on arguments that better evidence can answer.

Common grounds include claims that the injury happened outside work, that it was a pre-existing condition, or that the claim was lodged late. Each can often be contested with the right medical opinion and witness accounts from the worksite.

AMK prepares the review application, gathers the supporting material and deals with WorkCover Queensland or the self-insured employer for you.

04

What Happens When You Receive A Notice Of Assessment?

The notice sets out your degree of permanent impairment (DPI) and any statutory lump sum offer. A lump sum is offered when your DPI is assessed at 1% or more.

If your DPI is under 20%, or you have no DPI, you must choose between accepting the lump sum and keeping the right to sue your employer for common law damages. At 20% or more you can take the lump sum and still bring a common law claim. Separate DPIs, such as a physical and a psychiatric assessment, cannot be added together to reach 20%.

Because that choice can be permanent, get advice before you sign anything. You can also defer the decision while you work out which path is better for you.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions.

How long do I have to lodge a WorkCover claim in Toowoomba?

Lodge your claim with WorkCover Queensland within 6 months of the injury or diagnosis. Late claims can sometimes be accepted with a reasonable excuse, but it is far safer to lodge early.

Can I challenge a rejected WorkCover claim from Toowoomba?

Yes. You can apply for a review of the decision, generally within 3 months. AMK prepares the application and the supporting evidence for you.

Do I have to accept the lump sum in my Notice of Assessment?

No. If your DPI is under 20%, accepting the lump sum means giving up a common law claim, so get advice first. You can defer the decision while you consider your options.

Do I need to travel to Brisbane or Ipswich to see AMK?

No. We act for Toowoomba and Darling Downs workers by phone, video and home or hospital visits. Our office at Augustine Heights is there if you would like to meet in person.

Are seasonal and farm workers covered by WorkCover?

Workers injured in the course of their employment are generally covered, including seasonal, casual and farm workers. How your weekly earnings are calculated can vary, so check your payments carefully.
