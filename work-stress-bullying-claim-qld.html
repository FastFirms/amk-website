Not every work injury shows up on an X-ray. Sometimes it's the sleepless nights, the dread on Sunday evenings, the panic in the car park before your shift. Maybe it came from bullying, a traumatic incident, or months of pressure that finally broke something.

If your mental health has been seriously affected by your work, you might be wondering whether WorkCover covers it. As workers compensation lawyers serving Ipswich and Springfield, we hear this question often, usually from people who feel they "should just cope". You shouldn't have to. Here's how psychological injury claims work in Queensland.

This information applies to Queensland. Workers' compensation rules are different in other states and under Commonwealth schemes.

Does WorkCover cover psychological injuries?

Yes, it can. Queensland's workers' compensation scheme can cover psychological and psychiatric injuries, like depression, anxiety disorders and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), where work is a significant contributing factor to the injury. Some important exclusions apply, and these claims often need stronger evidence.

Under current Queensland law, work must generally be a significant contributing factor to the psychological or psychiatric injury. That's the same test used for physical injuries. It doesn't have to be the only cause, but it has to be more than a minor one.

You'll also need a diagnosis of a recognised psychological or psychiatric condition from a doctor, psychologist or psychiatrist. Feeling stressed, as real as that is, isn't enough on its own for a claim.

What kinds of situations lead to psychological injury claims?

They come from all sorts of workplaces. Common examples include workplace bullying or harassment, exposure to a traumatic event, unreasonable workloads over a long period, and violence or threats at work. Each claim depends on its facts and on medical evidence linking the condition to work.

Some situations we often see:

  • Bullying and harassment by a manager, co-worker or group
  • Traumatic incidents, such as witnessing a serious accident, being assaulted, or dealing with a death at work
  • Sexual harassment
  • Ongoing excessive work demands without support
  • Psychological injury following a physical injury, such as depression after a serious back injury at work

The greater Ipswich and Springfield area has a lot of people working in health care, education, logistics, retail and the public sector. Those industries can involve high-pressure environments, shift work and frontline exposure to distressing events.

What is the "reasonable management action" exclusion?

This is the most common reason psychological claims are rejected. Under Queensland law, a psychological injury generally isn't covered if it arose from reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way. Examples include a fair performance review, a properly run disciplinary process, or a reasonable decision about your job.

It's important to understand what this means and what it doesn't.

It doesn't mean any claim involving your manager is excluded. The action must be reasonable, and it must be carried out in a reasonable way. If a performance process was unfair, humiliating, or used as a form of bullying, the exclusion may not apply.

Typical examples of management action include:

  • performance appraisals and warnings
  • disciplinary processes and investigations
  • transfers, restructures and redundancy decisions
  • decisions about promotions or leave

Insurers often rely on this exclusion. That doesn't mean it applies in your case. The details of what happened, and how it happened, really matter.

How long do I have to lodge a WorkCover claim?

Generally, you need to lodge your application within six months of when your entitlement to compensation arises, which is usually linked to when a doctor first assesses your injury. There are limited exceptions. Lodging early helps, because it supports your claim and gets support started sooner.

With psychological injuries, people often wait. They hope it'll pass, or they worry about how a claim will look. We understand that. But delays can make claims harder. If your doctor has said your condition is connected to work, it's worth looking into your options without waiting.

You'll usually need:

  • a work capacity certificate from your treating doctor
  • a completed claim application to WorkCover Queensland or your employer's self-insurer
  • details of the events at work that caused your injury

What can I receive if my claim is accepted?

An accepted claim can give you weekly payments if you can't work, plus medical and treatment costs. That can include psychology or psychiatry sessions, GP appointments, medication and rehabilitation. These are called "statutory benefits". They're paid under the scheme without needing to prove anyone was at fault.

Weekly payment amounts are worked out by formula and can reduce over time. Medical and treatment costs need to be reasonable and related to your work injury.

Your claim may also involve a return-to-work plan. For a psychological injury, that might mean changed duties, different hours, or working away from the person or situation that caused your injury. You can raise concerns if a proposed plan doesn't feel safe for your mental health.

What if my claim is rejected?

You can ask for the decision to be reviewed. In Queensland, decisions by WorkCover or a self-insurer can generally be reviewed by the Workers' Compensation Regulator. Strict time limits apply to lodging a review, so act quickly. A rejection is often not the end of the road.

Psychological claims are rejected more often than physical injury claims, usually because of:

  • the reasonable management action exclusion
  • arguments that work wasn't a significant contributing factor
  • not enough medical evidence

A review lets an independent body look at the decision again, and you can provide more information. If the review doesn't go your way, there may be further appeal options. Getting advice before you lodge a review can help you put your best case forward.

Can I also make a common law claim?

Possibly. Separately from statutory benefits, an injured worker in Queensland may be able to make a common law claim for damages if their employer was negligent, for example by failing to address known bullying. This is a different process, with its own requirements and time limits.

Common law claims for psychological injury can be complex. You'll generally need medical evidence about the level of your impairment, and you'll need to show your employer breached its duty of care. A lawyer can help you work out whether this path is open to you.

What should I keep a record of?

Good records make a real difference in psychological injury claims. Because these claims often turn on what happened and when, notes made at the time can carry a lot of weight. Start keeping a simple record now, even if you haven't decided whether to claim.

Useful things to keep:

  • a diary of incidents, with dates, times, what was said and who was there
  • emails, texts and messages connected to what happened
  • any complaints you made at work, and the responses
  • appointment dates with your GP, psychologist or psychiatrist

Keep copies somewhere private, not only on a work computer or work phone.

Will making a claim affect my job?

The law protects workers from being sacked because they've made, or might make, a workers' compensation claim. Even so, many people worry about how a claim will affect their relationships at work. That's a normal feeling. Getting advice can help you understand your rights and protections.

If you're thinking about resigning because work is too much, talk to someone first if you can. Resigning may affect some aspects of your claim and your options.

If work has taken a toll on your mental health, reaching out can feel like a big step. You don't need to have it all figured out first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim WorkCover for stress in Queensland?

You may be able to, if a doctor diagnoses a recognised psychological or psychiatric condition and work was a significant contributing factor. General stress on its own usually isn't enough. Claims arising from reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way are generally excluded. Each claim depends on its own facts and medical evidence.

Can I claim for workplace bullying?

Yes, bullying is one of the most common causes of psychological injury claims in Queensland. You'll need a diagnosis and evidence linking your condition to the bullying. Keep notes of incidents, dates, witnesses and any emails or messages. Insurers may argue it was reasonable management action, so the details of what happened matter.

How long does a psychological injury claim take?

It varies. WorkCover Queensland generally aims to make decisions within set timeframes, but psychological claims often take longer because the insurer may interview witnesses and your employer. Getting your medical evidence and claim paperwork right at the start can help avoid delays.

Does my employer find out I've made a claim?

Yes. Your employer will be told about your claim, and the insurer will usually ask for their version of events. That's part of the process. Your private medical details are handled under privacy rules, but your employer will know a claim has been made.

Can I see my own psychologist?

Generally, yes. If your claim is accepted, the insurer can cover reasonable treatment from appropriate treating practitioners, including psychologists and psychiatrists. Check with the insurer about approvals before ongoing treatment, so you're not left out of pocket.
