What Is A Public Liability Claim In Brisbane?

Public liability law in Queensland requires businesses, property owners and local councils to take reasonable steps to ensure the safety of people on their premises or in spaces they control. Where they fail to do so and someone is injured as a result, a public liability claim may arise.

Common Brisbane public liability incidents include slips and falls in supermarkets, shopping centres and restaurants caused by wet or uneven floors; injuries caused by poorly maintained footpaths, parks or public spaces managed by Brisbane City Council; falls from inadequate lighting or signage; and injuries in commercial or recreational premises. 02

Who Can Be Held Liable For Brisbane Public Liability Incidents?

Liability in Brisbane public liability claims depends on who controlled the premises or space where the injury occurred and whether that party breached their duty of care.

Parties who may be liable include retail operators and shopping centre management, the Brisbane City Council and other local councils for footpath and public space maintenance, restaurant and cafe operators, gym and recreational facility owners, strata and body corporate managers for common areas, and commercial landlords. Identifying the correct respondent — and gathering evidence to establish breach — is a key legal task that benefits from early legal advice.

03

What Compensation Is Available For Brisbane Public Liability Claims?

Public liability compensation in Queensland can cover medical and rehabilitation expenses, past and future loss of income, care and assistance costs, and general damages for pain, suffering and loss of amenity.

The compensation available depends on the severity of the injury, the claimant's age and employment circumstances, and the strength of evidence establishing the duty of care, breach and causation. Brisbane residents in professional, trade and retail roles may have significant economic loss claims if their injuries affect their capacity to work.

04

Time Limits For Brisbane Public Liability Claims

The general limitation period for commencing public liability proceedings in Queensland is 3 years from the date of the incident. However, earlier procedural steps are required under the Personal Injuries Proceedings Act 2002, including pre-court notice requirements.

Evidence preservation is also critical. CCTV footage from Brisbane retail and commercial premises is typically overwritten within 30 days. Incident reports and witness details should be gathered immediately. Engaging a lawyer promptly maximises the evidence available to support your claim.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions.

What is the time limit for a Brisbane public liability claim?

The general limitation period is 3 years from the date of the incident. However, pre-court notice requirements under the PIPA apply earlier. CCTV evidence is typically overwritten within 30 days — act promptly to preserve it.

Can I claim for a slip and fall in a Brisbane shopping centre?

Yes. Shopping centre operators owe a duty of care to customers. If a slip or fall was caused by a wet floor, uneven surface or another hazard the operator knew or should have known about, a public liability claim may arise.

What if the Brisbane City Council is responsible for my injury?

Local councils owe a duty of care for the maintenance of footpaths, parks and public spaces. Claims against councils have specific procedural requirements. AMK handles council liability claims across Greater Brisbane.

Does No Win No Fee apply to Brisbane public liability claims?

Yes. All public liability claims handled by AMK are taken on a No Win, No Fee basis, with all disbursements advanced throughout the matter.

What compensation can I receive for a Brisbane public liability injury?

Compensation can cover medical and rehabilitation expenses, income loss, care and assistance costs, and general damages for pain and suffering. The amount depends on injury severity and the strength of the evidence establishing the occupier's liability.
