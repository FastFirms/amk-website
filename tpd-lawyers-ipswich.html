01

What Is TPD Insurance And Do Ipswich Residents Have It?

Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) insurance is included as default cover in most Australian superannuation accounts. Many account holders are unaware that this cover exists or what it provides.

If you are an Ipswich resident who has become unable to work due to injury or illness, there is a reasonable chance that your superannuation fund holds a TPD policy that you could claim against. The existence of the cover, the sum insured, and the definition of "total and permanent disability" that applies all depend on the specific fund and policy. AMK Compensation Lawyers can check your superannuation fund details on your behalf during a free consultation to determine whether a TPD claim is available to you — at no cost and with no obligation.

02

What Does "Total And Permanent Disability" Mean For A Claim?

The definition of "total and permanent disability" is not uniform — it varies from policy to policy and is the central issue in most TPD disputes.

Some policies require that the claimant is unable to ever work in their own occupation — the job they held at the time of the disability. Others apply a broader test, requiring that the claimant is unable to work in any occupation reasonably suited to their education, training and experience. The definition that applies to your policy determines what medical evidence is needed and how the insurer will assess your claim. This is precisely where legal expertise makes a material difference: understanding the exact definition and building the evidence to meet it is the core of what TPD lawyers do.

03

What TPD Compensation May Ipswich Claimants Receive?

A successful TPD claim results in payment of a lump sum by the insurer, which is paid through the superannuation fund. The amount depends on the sum insured under the policy, which varies from fund to fund and account to account.

Importantly, a TPD payout is entirely separate from and in addition to any workers compensation entitlements or CTP damages claim arising from the same injury or illness. Holding multiple superannuation accounts — whether current or former — can also mean holding multiple TPD policies, each of which may support a separate claim. The interactions between these claims and their tax treatment on withdrawal can be complex, and legal advice in advance of any withdrawal decisions is important.

04

How Do You Make A TPD Claim In Queensland And What Are The Time Limits?

A TPD claim is made by lodging an application with the superannuation fund trustee, supported by medical evidence from treating and, often, independent specialists. The fund passes the application to the insurer for assessment.

Time limits in TPD matters can be strict and vary by policy. Some policies contain limitation clauses that extinguish the right to claim after a specified period from the date the disability first occurred. This means that delay — even for a matter of months — can result in a valid claim being denied on limitation grounds alone. Acting promptly once you become unable to work, or once you are advised that your incapacity may be permanent, is strongly recommended. A free policy check by AMK Compensation Lawyers will identify any applicable time limits in your specific policy.

How Do I Find Out What Superannuation Funds I Have And Whether They Include TPD Cover?

Many Australians have lost or forgotten superannuation accounts — particularly those who changed jobs, worked casually, or moved interstate. The ATO's online services (via myGov) allow you to search for all super accounts held in your name.

Once you have identified your funds, the product disclosure statement (PDS) for each fund sets out whether default insurance cover — including TPD — is included and what the sum insured is. In practice, reading and interpreting PDS documents and TPD policy definitions can be complex. AMK Compensation Lawyers will check your funds on your behalf during a free consultation: we identify all policies that may support a claim, explain the definition that applies under each policy, and give you an honest assessment of the likelihood of success — at no cost and with no obligation. It is not unusual to discover TPD cover that has never been claimed, including in old accounts from earlier employment.

07

What Are The Most Common Reasons TPD Claims Are Rejected And How Can They Be Challenged?

The most common grounds for TPD claim rejection are: the claimant does not meet the policy's definition of "total and permanent disability"; insufficient medical evidence; and disputes about whether the disability is permanent rather than temporary.

Insurers also frequently reject claims on the basis that the claimant could theoretically perform some form of work — even sedentary or part-time work entirely different from their pre-injury occupation — relying on the broader "any occupation" definition rather than the "own occupation" definition that may actually apply under the claimant's policy. A rejected TPD claim can be challenged through the super fund's internal dispute resolution process, the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA), or, in appropriate cases, through court proceedings. The outcome of a challenge often depends heavily on the quality and framing of the medical evidence provided. Our lawyers work with treating specialists and, where necessary, independent medical assessors to build the evidence base needed to overturn a rejection. There is no cost to pursue a challenge on a No Win, No Fee basis.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions.

How do I know if I have TPD cover in my superannuation?

Many Australians are unsure whether they hold TPD insurance and, if so, what it covers. AMK Compensation Lawyers can check your superannuation fund details for you at no charge during a free consultation. In most cases, default insurance — including TPD cover — is included in standard superannuation accounts without the member needing to elect it separately.

Can I make a TPD claim and also claim workers compensation?

These claims can potentially run in parallel, but there are complex interactions between TPD lump sums, WorkCover benefits and common law damages that require careful management. The sequencing of claims and the timing of payments can affect overall entitlements. Legal advice is essential to coordinate the claims correctly.

My insurer rejected my TPD claim — can I dispute it?

Yes. A rejected TPD claim can be reviewed and challenged through a number of formal processes, including internal complaint procedures with the super fund, the Australian Financial Complaints Authority, and, in appropriate cases, court proceedings. Legal assistance in disputing a declined claim often makes a significant difference to the outcome.

Is there a time limit on TPD claims for Ipswich residents?

Time limits on TPD claims vary by policy and insurer. Some policies impose strict limitation periods from the date of the disability event, and others have conditions that can affect whether a claim is valid at all after a certain period. It is important to act promptly and seek legal advice early — ideally as soon as you become aware that you may be unable to return to work.
