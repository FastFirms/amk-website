How Long Does a WorkCover Claim Take in Queensland?

That is the question every injured worker asks, usually within days of lodging. The honest answer is: it depends which part of the process you're asking about. The initial claim decision has a legal deadline. The DPI assessment waits for your injury to stabilise. A common law claim moves at the pace of negotiations and, sometimes, the courts. This article sets out what each stage involves, what determines how long it takes, and what you can do to avoid unnecessary delays.

Stage 1: The initial claim decision — 20 business days

Under section 134 of the Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003 (Qld), WorkCover Queensland must decide your claim within 20 business days of receiving it. If WorkCover needs further information from you, your employer or your doctor, it may extend this period — but it must tell you that it is doing so and must still decide as soon as reasonably practicable.

In practice, straightforward claims with clear medical evidence and no employer dispute are often decided in 10 to 15 business days. If your claim involves a pre-existing condition, a disputed mechanism of injury, or an employer who contests liability, the initial decision may take longer and there is a greater risk of rejection.

Once a claim is accepted, your statutory entitlements — weekly compensation and reasonable medical treatment — start immediately. If your claim is rejected, you have the right to seek a review through WorkCover and then the Queensland Industrial Relations Commission.

Stage 2: Weekly income replacement — ongoing while incapacitated

Once your claim is accepted, you should receive your first weekly payment within two to three business days. WorkCover pays income replacement at 85% of your normal weekly earnings for the first 26 weeks of incapacity. From week 27, the calculation changes: you receive 75% of your normal weekly earnings if you have no capacity for any work, or a reduced amount calculated on the difference between your capacity earnings and your normal earnings if you have some capacity.

Weekly compensation continues until one of the following happens:

  • You return to full pre-injury duties
  • You reach your maximum entitlement period
  • Your employer makes a suitable duties offer that you accept or unreasonably refuse
  • You reach the age of entitlement for the pension (for older workers)

WorkCover will periodically review your capacity for work, usually every few months, based on updated medical certificates and sometimes an independent medical examination. These reviews are part of the normal process — they are not automatically a sign that your entitlements are about to be cut.

Practical tip

Keep your treating doctor updated on every symptom that is limiting your work capacity. WorkCover's income replacement decisions are based heavily on medical certificates. A certificate that understates your restrictions can lead to your entitlement being reduced or ended prematurely.

Stage 3: Medical treatment — as long as the claim is active

WorkCover covers reasonable and necessary medical treatment for the duration of your claim. This includes GP visits, specialist appointments, physiotherapy, psychology, hospital treatment, surgery, and aids or equipment if required. There is no fixed end date for medical coverage — it continues while your treatment is reasonably required as a result of your work injury.

WorkCover may request prior approval for certain treatment, particularly surgery or high-cost items. If approval is refused, you can seek a review. Do not delay treatment while waiting for approval decisions — speak to your treating doctor and your lawyer about how to manage the timing.

Stage 4: Degree of permanent impairment (DPI) assessment — typically 6 to 18 months post-injury

Once your injury has stabilised — meaning your condition has plateaued and further treatment is unlikely to significantly improve it — WorkCover will arrange for an independent medical examiner to assess your degree of permanent impairment (DPI). This percentage assessment determines your entitlement to a statutory lump sum and, critically, your options for a common law claim.

The assessment usually takes about an hour. The examiner will review your medical records, examine you, and apply the guidelines set out in the WorkCover Queensland Guidelines for Evaluation of Permanent Impairment. You will typically receive the written report within two to four weeks.

If your DPI is less than 20% (or you have no DPI), you face an important choice: you can either accept a statutory lump sum based on your DPI, or you can pursue a common law damages claim — but not both. If your DPI is 20% or above, you can receive a statutory impairment lump sum and still pursue a common law claim. That distinction is significant, and the financial consequences of choosing incorrectly can be substantial. Do not make that decision without legal advice.

If you disagree with the DPI assessment, you have the right to request a review through WorkCover. If the dispute is not resolved, it can be referred to the Medical Assessment Tribunal.

Stage 5: Common law claim — typically 12 to 36 months

A common law claim is not available to every worker. You need to establish that your employer (or another person) was negligent and that their negligence caused or materially contributed to your injury. If you can establish negligence, you may be able to recover past and future economic loss, the cost of past and future medical and care expenses, and general damages for pain and suffering — amounts that can be substantially greater than your statutory entitlements.

The common law process in Queensland is governed by Chapter 5 of the Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003 and involves several steps before proceedings can be issued:

  1. Notice of claim — formally notifying the insurer of your intention to make a claim, triggering time for investigation
  2. Exchange of documents and investigation — both sides gather medical evidence, employment records, and evidence relevant to liability
  3. Compulsory conference — a mandatory dispute resolution step before court; many claims settle here
  4. Proceedings, if necessary — if the compulsory conference does not resolve the matter, proceedings can be issued in the District or Supreme Court

The majority of common law WorkCover claims in Queensland settle without a trial, often at or shortly after the compulsory conference. A straightforward matter where liability is clear and both sides have exchanged updated medical evidence may settle within 12 to 18 months of the notice of claim. Complex cases involving significant quantum disputes, multiple defendants, or delayed recovery can take two to three years or longer.

Time limits you cannot afford to miss
  • Lodging your WorkCover claim: you should lodge within six months of the injury date. After six months, WorkCover may still accept it, but only if there is a reasonable excuse for the delay.
  • Common law claim: the limitation period is generally three years from the date of injury under the Limitation of Actions Act 1974 (Qld). Extensions may be available in some circumstances, but do not rely on this.
  • Appealing a rejected claim: if WorkCover rejects your claim, you have a fixed time to seek a review. That deadline is printed on the rejection letter — act immediately.

What causes WorkCover claims to take longer

Several factors consistently extend claim timelines:

  • Disputed liability — if WorkCover or your employer disputes that the injury happened at work, or that employment was a significant contributing factor, the initial decision period extends and the claim may need to go through dispute resolution
  • Pre-existing conditions — insurers investigate prior injuries or degenerative conditions; clear medical evidence linking your work injury to your current condition is essential from the outset
  • Independent medical examinations (IMEs) — WorkCover can arrange its own medical examinations; if the IME report differs significantly from your treating doctor's view, that can lead to disputes about treatment approvals or return-to-work decisions
  • Delayed DPI assessment — if your injury does not stabilise, the DPI assessment is postponed; this is particularly common with psychological injuries and chronic pain conditions
  • Gaps in medical treatment — unexplained gaps in treatment can be used to argue that your injury had resolved or that your ongoing incapacity is not related to work

What you can do to keep your claim moving

Many of the delays that frustrate WorkCover claimants are avoidable:

  • Lodge your claim as soon as possible — do not wait to see how the injury develops
  • Report the injury to your employer in writing, the same day if possible
  • See your treating doctor regularly and ensure your certificates are current
  • Keep copies of everything — forms, letters, certificates, medical reports
  • Tell your lawyer about any changes in your condition, your employment, or your treatment
  • Do not give recorded statements to WorkCover, your employer's insurer, or investigators without speaking to a lawyer first
  • Get legal advice before accepting any lump sum or settlement offer

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does WorkCover Queensland have to decide my claim?

WorkCover Queensland must make an initial decision within 20 business days of receiving your claim. If WorkCover needs more information from you, your employer or your doctor, it must tell you and can extend this period — but it must still decide as soon as practicable. If your claim has not been decided and you have not been told why, speak to a lawyer.

How long does it take to receive WorkCover weekly payments?

Once your claim is accepted, weekly compensation should start within two to three business days. It is paid at 85% of your normal weekly earnings for the first 26 weeks of incapacity, then calculated differently from week 27 depending on your capacity for work.

How long does a WorkCover DPI assessment take?

The degree of permanent impairment (DPI) assessment happens when your injury has stabilised — usually six to 18 months after the injury. The assessment itself takes about an hour. You will usually receive the written assessment result within two to four weeks of the examination.

How long does a common law WorkCover claim take in Queensland?

Most common law claims in Queensland resolve within 12 to 24 months of the notice of claim being given. Complex cases can take longer. The process includes compulsory pre-court procedures. Many claims settle at or before the compulsory conference without a trial.

What is the time limit for making a WorkCover claim in Queensland?

You should lodge your WorkCover claim within six months of the date of injury. After six months, WorkCover may still accept it if there is a reasonable excuse. For a common law claim, the limitation period is generally three years from the date of injury. If you are approaching either of these limits, speak to a lawyer immediately.
