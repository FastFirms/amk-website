How Does Queensland CTP Insurance Work For Brisbane Accidents?

CTP — Compulsory Third Party insurance — is mandatory for every registered Queensland vehicle. It provides compensation to people injured by the negligent driving of the insured vehicle. Your claim is made against the at-fault driver's CTP insurer, not against the driver personally.

Under the Queensland CTP scheme, the single most important deadline is lodging your notice of claim within 9 months of the accident. Failure to give notice within this time can permanently affect your ability to claim general damages for pain and suffering. Our CTP lawyers ensure notice is lodged promptly and correctly.

03

What Compensation Can Brisbane Car Accident Victims Claim?

CTP compensation in Queensland covers medical and rehabilitation expenses, past and future loss of income and superannuation, the cost of care and assistance, and general damages for pain, suffering and loss of amenity.

For Brisbane workers in professional, trade, retail and healthcare roles, economic loss claims — covering lost wages and future earning capacity — can form the most substantial component of a serious injury claim. Superannuation contributions lost during a period of incapacity are also compensable. The total compensation available depends on the severity of injury, age, employment circumstances and the quality of medical and economic evidence assembled.

04

Time Limits For Brisbane Car Accident Claims

The 9-month notice requirement is the most critical deadline for Queensland CTP claims. Under the Motor Accident Insurance Act 1994, you must give notice of your claim to the at-fault driver's CTP insurer within 9 months of the accident.

The general limitation period for commencing proceedings is 3 years from the date of the accident. However, a claim that has not had a proper notice lodged at the 9-month mark may be significantly restricted even within this window. CCTV footage from Brisbane intersections is typically overwritten within 30 days, making early action critical for preserving evidence.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions.

What is the time limit to claim after a Brisbane car accident?

You must give notice of your CTP claim to the at-fault driver's insurer within 9 months of the accident. This is a strict requirement in most circumstances. The general limitation period for commencing proceedings is 3 years from the accident date. Do not delay — evidence such as CCTV footage is often overwritten within weeks.

Can I still claim if the Brisbane accident was partly my fault?

Contributory negligence reduces but does not necessarily eliminate your entitlement. Queensland law allows for apportionment of fault, meaning you may still receive compensation for the portion of the accident attributable to the other party's negligence.

Does AMK handle accidents on Brisbane motorways like the M1 or Gateway?

Yes. AMK Compensation Lawyers handles CTP and motor vehicle injury claims arising from accidents anywhere in Queensland, including the M1 Pacific Motorway, Gateway Motorway, Logan Motorway, Inner City Bypass and all Brisbane suburban roads.

What if the at-fault Brisbane driver was uninsured or unidentified?

Queensland's Nominal Defendant scheme provides a claims mechanism where the at-fault vehicle was unregistered or cannot be identified. Strict time limits and procedures apply — seek legal advice promptly if this may apply to your situation.

Is a police report required for a Brisbane car accident claim?

A police report is not always a formal requirement but is strongly advisable. It creates an objective contemporaneous record of the accident and can be valuable evidence in the claims process.
