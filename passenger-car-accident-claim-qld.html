You didn't choose the route. You weren't holding the wheel. You were just along for the ride, maybe heading home along the Centenary Highway or doing the school run through Springfield Lakes, and now you're dealing with pain, appointments and time off work.

If that's you, here's the good news first: in Queensland, passengers are often in one of the strongest positions to make an injury claim. As car accident lawyers based in Greater Springfield, we talk with passengers all the time who assume they can't claim, usually because the driver was their partner, their mum or their mate. This guide walks you through how it actually works, in plain English.

This information applies to Queensland. The rules in other states are different.

Can I claim if I was a passenger in a car accident?

Yes, usually. In Queensland, a passenger hurt in a crash can generally claim against the compulsory third party (CTP) insurer of the vehicle whose driver was at fault, wholly or partly. You don't need to prove you did anything right, because you weren't driving. The main question is which driver caused the crash.

CTP insurance is the compulsory insurance that comes with every Queensland vehicle registration. It exists to cover people injured because of how a vehicle was driven. It doesn't cover damage to cars. That's a separate insurance policy.

Queensland's CTP scheme is "fault-based". In plain terms, that means a claim is made against the insurer of the driver who caused the accident. As a passenger, you're rarely the one at fault, so your claim often comes down to identifying whose insurer is responsible.

What if the driver was my partner, family member or friend?

This is the question we hear most. If the person driving your car caused the crash, your claim is usually against their vehicle's CTP insurer, not against them personally. You're not suing your partner or your friend out of their own pocket. The insurer deals with the claim.

We understand the hesitation. Nobody wants to feel like they're blaming someone they love. But the CTP system was designed for exactly this situation. The driver's registration paid for that cover, and it's there to protect the people travelling in their car.

A few things are worth knowing:

  • The driver may need to help with information, like confirming what happened and their insurer details.
  • Your claim doesn't come out of their savings. The CTP insurer pays approved claims.

If you're worried about how a claim might affect someone close to you, raise it in your first chat with a lawyer. It's a normal question and there's no pressure.

What if another driver caused the crash?

Then your claim is usually against the other vehicle's CTP insurer. If both drivers share the blame, the insurers may share responsibility too. As a passenger, you generally don't need to sort out who's more at fault yourself, but it does affect which insurer handles your claim.

What matters for you is that the crash is properly documented. That means:

  • the accident reported to police where required
  • the registration numbers of every vehicle involved
  • names and contact details of drivers and witnesses
  • photos of the scene, vehicles and your injuries, if you can safely take them

Couldn't get those details? A lawyer can often help track them down later.

What if the at-fault car was unregistered or drove off?

You may still be able to claim. In Queensland, a body called the Nominal Defendant handles claims where the at-fault vehicle was unregistered or uninsured, or can't be identified, such as a hit-and-run. These claims have extra requirements and can be time-sensitive, so it's best to get advice early.

For a hit-and-run, you'll generally need to show you made proper efforts to identify the vehicle, so reporting to police promptly is especially important. The notice deadlines for Nominal Defendant claims can be shorter than for regular CTP claims. Don't wait to find out.

How long do I have to make a passenger claim in Queensland?

Not long, and there's more than one deadline. Queensland has an early deadline to formally notify the CTP insurer of your claim. It's generally the earlier of nine months after the accident (or after symptoms first appear) and one month after you first consult a lawyer about claiming. There's also a longer overall deadline to start court proceedings. Missing either can put your claim at serious risk.

What can a passenger claim for?

That depends on your injuries and how they affect your life. In general terms, a Queensland CTP claim may cover things like medical and rehabilitation costs, lost income now and in future, help you need around the home, and an amount for pain and suffering. Every claim is different, and nobody can promise you a figure.

Some costs may be covered early in your claim, before it's settled. For example, the insurer may agree to pay for reasonable treatment and rehabilitation once your claim is accepted.

Things that can make a real difference to your claim:

  • Seeing a doctor early, even if you feel "mostly okay". Some injuries, like whiplash or concussion, show up days later.
  • Keeping receipts for medication, physio, travel to appointments and anything else you've paid.
  • Keeping a simple diary of pain, missed work and things you can't do. It sounds small, but it helps.

Does it matter that I wasn't wearing a seatbelt?

It can. If you weren't wearing a seatbelt, or you got in a car knowing the driver was affected by alcohol or drugs, the insurer may argue that you contributed to your own injuries. That can reduce the compensation you receive. It doesn't automatically end your claim.

This is called "contributory negligence". It means you're found partly responsible for your own injuries, and your compensation is reduced to reflect that. How much it's reduced depends on the facts.

Please don't let worries about this stop you from asking questions. Be honest with your lawyer about what happened. They can explain how it might affect your claim and what your options are.

What about passengers in Uber, taxis or buses?

Passengers in rideshare cars, taxis and buses can generally claim through CTP too, in the same way as passengers in a private car. The claim is usually against the CTP insurer of whichever vehicle was at fault. If you were hurt boarding or leaving a bus, the rules can be more complicated, so get advice.

Do I really need a lawyer for a passenger claim?

You don't have to use one, but many people find it helps. The CTP insurer is not on your side, and the paperwork, deadlines and medical evidence can be a lot to handle while you're recovering. A lawyer can manage the claim, deal with the insurer and help make sure nothing important is missed.

The Motor Accident Insurance Commission (MAIC), the Queensland regulator for CTP, has helpful information on its website if you'd like to read more about the process yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim if my husband or wife was driving and caused the accident?

Yes, usually. In Queensland, your claim would generally be made against the CTP insurer of the car your spouse was driving, not against your spouse personally. The insurer handles the claim and pays any approved compensation. It's a common situation, and the CTP scheme is designed to cover it. Get advice early, as strict deadlines apply.

Do I have to go to court to get compensation?

Most Queensland CTP claims are resolved without a court hearing. There's a pre-court process where information is exchanged, and parties usually try to settle at a compulsory conference. Court proceedings are only needed if the claim can't be resolved. Even then, many claims still settle before trial.

What if I only had minor injuries at first?

You should still see a doctor and keep a record. Some injuries, like whiplash, back pain or psychological effects, get worse over days or weeks. A medical record made soon after the crash helps link your injuries to the accident. Notification deadlines still apply even if your injuries seem minor at first.

Can a child passenger make a claim?

Yes. A child injured as a passenger in Queensland can generally make a claim, usually with a parent or guardian acting for them. Special time-limit rules can apply to children, but it's still best to get advice early while evidence and witness memories are fresh.

Will making a claim affect the driver's insurance premium?

CTP insurance in Queensland is paid as part of vehicle registration, and premiums aren't set the way ordinary car insurance premiums are. A CTP claim is separate from the driver's comprehensive car insurance. If you're unsure, ask the driver to check with their own insurer.
